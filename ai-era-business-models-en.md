# Business Models in the AI Era

> Created 2026-06-27

## The Core Shift: The Value Anchor Moves from Deliverables to Judgment

The traditional model sells solutions — CRM systems, consulting reports, architecture proposals. The buyer pays for "owning this thing," and the risk lies with the buyer.

In the AI era, execution cost approaches zero, and the solution itself depreciates. The value anchor moves to the outcome, and the risk shifts to the seller. But outcomes are hard to price and their boundaries are blurry, so "selling outcomes" is more a direction than a directly deployable model.

A more precise description: **What the AI era sells is judgment and trust. Execution is free, judgment is paid for, trust commands a premium.**

## "Ownership → Usage Rights" Is Not Just the Old SaaS Narrative

The usage right in traditional SaaS is renting a fixed product. The usage right in AI is renting a capability that learns your context — the longer you use it, the better it understands your business logic, preferences, and historical decisions.

Key changes:
- **Exit costs grow non-linearly** — migration is not just a data export; it also includes the cold start of "a new system re-understanding me"
- **The pricing logic has changed** — from charging per seat/per usage to charging by depth of context
- **The competitive battlefield has shifted** — it is not how many features you have, but whose context is deeper and whose adaptive capability is stronger

The paradox: AI makes usage rights more valuable while also making the alternative to ownership cheaper (the cost of self-trained models is falling). The premise is that your model/context is better than what the user builds themselves.

## Five Structural Observations

### 1. Transaction Cost Collapse Redraws Market Boundaries

What AI eliminates is not production cost but coordination cost. The number of projects one person can manage goes from 3 to 30 — not because they became stronger, but because the friction disappeared.

### 2. Middlemen Are Redefined, Not Eliminated

Information asymmetry is eliminated by AI, but quality assurance and risk sharing have become more important. Things generated by AI all look "about equally good," so the cost of telling real from fake and good from bad rises. People who know their field are worth more.

### 3. The Pricing Unit Shifts from "Input" to "Risk Bearing"

AI does not bear consequences; people do. Whoever bears the consequences gets paid. "AI-assisted" is easier to commercialize than "AI-automated," because a human is in the loop, and human judgment is the carrier of risk.

### 4. Data Moats Weaken, Context Moats Strengthen

AI can extract 80% of the insight from public data, but "understanding a specific context" cannot be crawled; it can only be accumulated through long-term collaboration. The customer relationship changes from "retained because the service is good" to "I understand your context better than any alternative."

### 5. AI Makes Small and Beautiful Easier — and Easier to Die

The barrier to entry falls (one person doing the work of five), but the barrier to survival is unchanged (you still have to find a problem others cannot solve). AI is an equal-opportunity amplifier, not an unequal moat.
