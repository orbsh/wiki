# The AI Liquidity Camp and K-Shaped Salary Divergence

> **Languages:** [English](ai-liquidity-camp-en.md) (primary) · [中文](ai-liquidity-camp.md)
> Observed 2026-06. Capital stakes everything on AI as the only painless way out of the macroeconomic deadlock, forming an extremely concentrated flow of funds that in turn tears apart the salary structure.

## The Phenomenon: A Liquidity Camp

Early June 2026 US stock data:
- S&P 500: 7600+, Nasdaq: 27000+, Dow Jones: 51000+ (all at record highs)
- S&P up 9 weeks straight (longest since 2023)
- Nvidia +6% in a single day (new PC chip), Marvell +32.5% (one sentence from Jensen Huang — "the next trillion-dollar company"; best since its 2000 listing), HPE +20% (AI earnings), Philadelphia Semiconductor Index +6%
- **Same day**: Intel -4%, Google -4% (raising $80 billion to feed AI infrastructure, of which $10 billion comes from Buffett's Berkshire)

**Concentration**: Micron, Nvidia, and Google alone contribute 40%+ of the upward revision to the S&P 500's 2026 EPS estimate.

**The essence**: This is not a broad bull market; it is capital from all over the world panicking into AI — this one air-raid shelter — because the old economy (nowhere left to go under high rates and heavy debt) offers no exit. The share price is the most honest voting machine in this high-stakes gamble.

## The Macroeconomic Deadlock: Why There Is No Alternative to AI

Traditional magic has stopped working — the system no longer holds "leverage that has not been maxed out" and "credit that has not been overdrawn":
- Suppress inflation → raise rates and shrink the balance sheet, draining liquidity
- Keep debt from default → cut rates and print money, injecting liquidity
- **The two cannot be had at once**

History offers four exits (each one paid for by someone):
1. A Volcker-style hard puncture (1980s rates of 20%+, deep recession + a wave of defaults)
2. Financial repression (nominal rates held below inflation for long stretches, negative real rates, savers and fixed-wage earners quietly servicing the debt)
3. Hyperinflation (Weimar / Zimbabwe / Argentina; paper money turns into waste paper and resets everything)
4. **A productivity leap** (the only painless path: grow the pie to dilute the debt; technological progress is inherently anti-inflationary and hands central banks a step down to cut rates)

**Capital's bet**: that AI is the key to exit number four. Money flees the old economy (traditional energy, regional banks — clutching low-interest long-term Treasuries and commercial real estate loans that are underwater at high rates) and plunges headlong into the new riverbed of chips/compute.

## The Salary Formula

**Salary = position × leverage × irreplaceability**

Salary is not a reward for diligence / credentials / IQ; it is where you sit in the river of capital × your irreplaceability. Where money flows, the people there are lifted to the sky; where money flees, the people there are thrown off the train.

### Position: The AI Pyramid Hierarchy

From top to bottom:
1. Application layer (chatbots, AI assistants)
2. The large models themselves
3. Compute (Nvidia GPUs)
4. **Chip design / EDA tools** (globally monopolized by three players — Synopsys / Cadence / Siemens — with very few practitioners)
5. Wafer fabrication (TSMC)

The further downstream the money flows, the closer to the "chokehold" throat position, the higher the gold content per unit. No matter which application-layer player wins or loses, the more gold diggers there are, the more the shovel sellers earn.

**K-shaped tearing within the same industry**: both in semiconductors, whoever stands at the throat of AI compute soars, while whoever fails to squeeze into the throat is cast aside just the same (Nvidia vs. Intel).

### Leverage: The Multiplier by Which Output Is Amplified

A top AI researcher is worth tens of millions of dollars — not because he is 100 times smarter or more diligent than a middle-school teacher, but because his algorithms run on hundreds of thousands of GPUs serving billions of users, so that each unit of brainpower is multiplied by an astronomical coefficient.

A middle-school teacher's output is nearly linear with time invested (there is a ceiling on how many students can be taught in a year) and cannot be amplified by capital leverage. This has nothing to do with morality or how noble the contribution is; it turns only on whether the output can be pried by capital and amplified by technology.

Google raises $80 billion (even Buffett chips in $10 billion) to feed compute, because it firmly believes every dollar will be amplified into returns several or dozens of times over.

### Irreplaceability: Capabilities AI Cannot Supply

When AI turns codified knowledge into a free, unlimited supply, scarcity flips:
- **Taste and judgment**: the tacit knowledge to know which of ten thousand AI proposals is actually good
- **The ability to define problems**: AI is the strongest problem-solver but does not know which problem is worth solving; posing the right question is a hundred times scarcer than solving it
- **Domain depth**: muscle-memory-level understanding that can see through AI's earnest-sounding nonsense
- **The courage and temperament to shoulder responsibility**: AI will never answer for the consequences of a decision; people willing to make the call and sign their name will always be scarce
- **The ability to be present in the physical world**: requiring hands and a body, interpersonal trust and connection

## The Empirical Evidence of K-Shaped Tearing

### The Heaven End
- Meta poaching: a $300 million / 4-year compensation package (first year possibly over $100 million), rumored $100 million signing bonus (half true, half false)
- Researchers at top AI labs earn a solid tens of millions of dollars a year
- China: AI job postings have surged 29-fold from 1.5 years ago (Maimai, 2025.7); every 2 AI positions match only 1 suitable candidate
- Fresh PhDs from top universities approach 2 million RMB a year
- ByteDance's large-model foundation campus-hire standard package: 50k RMB/month × 15, the fiercer ones 80k monthly salary + 4 years of stock

### The Hell End (Same Industry, Same Major)
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab (ADP data): employment of 22-25-year-old software developers is down one-fifth from its late-2022 peak; employment of 30+ year-olds in the same field actually rose 6-12%
- US CS graduates' unemployment rate 6.1%, CE 7.5% (finding a job with a computer engineering degree is harder than with a fine arts degree)
- Entry-level tech job postings halved within one to two years
- Google/Meta new-grad hiring at half of 2021
- Overall unemployment rate for 22-27-year-olds: 7.4% (twice the national average)
- US new-grad "underemployment rate" reached 42.5% in 2025Q4 (highest since 2020)

**Starting salary comparison** (US class of 2025):
- Engineering average $78k, CS $76-85k, CE/SWE $82k
- Communications just over $60k, social sciences lower still
- Top vs. bottom majors: a $20k+ gap at the very first job, widening many times over by mid-career

**China's chain of contempt**: AI algorithms > backend > frontend/testing. From the same 985 master's degree, the first job differs by 3-5× depending on the direction.

## The Paradox of AI Eating Entry-Level Jobs

AI is best at "codified knowledge" — the kind with standard answers, repeatable, pattern-based: writing boilerplate code, routine debugging, organizing materials, following procedures. These are precisely the first few rungs by which junior engineers climb the career ladder.

- Within six quarters of a company adopting generative AI, junior-position employment drops 9-10%, while senior positions do not budge
- The job-hopping premium collapsed from nearly 20% in 2022 to 7% in mid-2025
- Salesforce stopped hiring junior positions entirely in 2025

**The paradox**: Today's senior experts climbed up from the rookies who once wrote boilerplate code. Saw off the entry rung → a senior talent shortage in 5-10 years → mass production of hollow architects who "only know how to direct AI and have never laid a brick with their own hands."

**The most dangerous position**: neither standing at the tens-of-millions-a-year throat, nor able to touch even the first rung because entry-level jobs have vanished — permanently stuck outside the door.

## The Fatal Misalignment of Education

What exam-oriented education (gaokao / graduate entrance exams / problem drills / certification) optimizes and rewards = the ability to be standardized, repeatable, and single-correct-answer = the skill AI learns fastest and does cheapest.

When AI turns "problem-solving" into a zero-marginal-cost free good, children who stake all their chips on "I am especially good at solving problems" win a fast ticket to a destiny of replacement.

**The fatal mistake**: attributing the gap to "not working hard enough" or "the school not being good enough." A child standing in the shallows may be more diligent, more obedient, and higher-scoring than one standing at the throat. The difference is not in the person, but in the flow speed of the river beneath their feet. Education uses the narrative of "effort" to cover up the truth of "position."

## Two Mistaken Postures

### Foolishly Rushing In
Treating "can use AI" as a moat. When everyone can call a large model, "can use AI" = basic literacy like reading and writing, and basic literacy generates no premium. A person skilled at writing prompts is still essentially standing in the shallows most easily flooded — when the next generation of models can generate its own prompts and plan tasks, he is the first to be swept away. Those who chase the waves are usually smashed dead on the beach by them.

### Cold Detachment / Self-Styled Superiority
"I disdain to keep company with machines; I hold fast to purity." Craftsmen in the steam-engine age who despised machines and clung to the dignity of handwork kept their respectability and also walked themselves into the museum. Refusing new tools = voluntarily standing on the wrong side of the tearing. The tuition for superiority may be a whole generation's position.

## The Correct Posture

Like an experienced craftsman regarding a new power tool — calmly and thoroughly master it as a lever that amplifies your own strength, but **never mistake the tool itself for a moat**.

- The first half: you must use it, use it as fluently as your native language, internalize it as part of your thinking (like English and search engines); this is the entry ticket, not the finish line
- The second half (the key): the money is not in "using AI"; the money is in "what AI cannot do, and what AI's cheapness makes all the scarcer by contrast"

## Five Recommendations

1. **English + AI = a thinking operating system** (not two courses). The two underlying interfaces for plugging into the world's most cutting-edge information flow and its strongest productivity. Being able to read the latest papers in English + being fluent at using AI to turn vague ideas into runnable prototypes = several times the cognitive bandwidth of being locked inside a Chinese exam-oriented system.

2. **Question-posing ability in exchange for problem-solving ability**. A fundamental shift in educational goals. Ask less "what is the answer," ask more "why is this thing worth doing" and "on what grounds should I believe this answer."

3. **Real work in exchange for drilled certificates**. In an AI-flooded world, certificates/transcripts depreciate fast (they prove exactly the abilities that can be standardized/machine-replicated). A 16-year-old with a few projects on GitHub that are actually used > a cabinet full of award certificates. Work is the hardest résumé of this era.

4. **One specialty with multiple abilities**: horizontal breadth (commanding AI to handle a thousand loose ends) + a vertical depth anchor (the confidence to not be deceived by AI and, in turn, to direct it). Breadth makes you an excellent "commander"; the vertical axis makes you an irreplaceable "expert." In an era when junior positions are sawed off, a vertical axis sunk in early is the key to bypassing the collapsed intersection and landing directly halfway up the mountain.

5. **Global mobility as a family skill**. The other end of the AI gamble is an undefused debt bomb. Google and its peers hitting new highs while frantically issuing debt to burn money building data centers = a breathtaking circular financing (giants borrow → chip companies book revenue → the index booms). If the AI productivity dividend lands before debt interest crushes everything, all is well; otherwise it is back to the three painful old paths (violent deleveraging / financial repression / hyperinflation), which means wealth redistribution and some currency/market/asset being slaughtered. Giving children the ability to live and work across borders, and the family's assets/identity room to maneuver across multiple jurisdictions = the most plain-spoken of hedges. Global mobility is itself a top-tier asset.

## The Ultimate Framework

The speed at which AI changes the world vs. the speed at which high interest rates crush the vast debt system. Nobody knows the ending. Capital stakes all its chips on AI landing before the old cycle collapses. Every ordinary person is an involuntary chip in this gamble.

**What can be decided**: which side of the tear you yourself stand on when the dust settles.

What this era rewards:
- ❌ The most diligent / most obedient / highest-scoring problem-driller (the machine moves faster and cheaper)
- ✅ The people best at turning tools into levers, with the sharpest judgment, the most courage to shoulder responsibility, and the largest room to maneuver kept for themselves and their families

The more omnipotent the machine becomes, the more valuable the person who understands "what is truly worth doing."
