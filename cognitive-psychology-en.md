# Cognitive Psychology and Personal Growth

> **Languages:** [English](cognitive-psychology-en.md) (primary) · [中文](cognitive-psychology.md)

*Principles of self-drive, habit formation, and cognitive growth.*

## The Origin of Self-Drive
- **Thesis**: Self-drive is rarely innate; it is "fed" through continuous positive feedback loops.
- **The loop**: `start → finish → feedback → see progress → willingness to repeat`.
- **Identity formation**: Repetition creates momentum; momentum solidifies into identity ("I am the kind of person who does this"). Once identity is stable, difficulty can rise naturally.

## The Three Laws of Growth
1. **Simplicity is the engine**: A low threshold triggers action. If the beginning is too hard, the loop breaks before it starts.
2. **Consistency is the compounding advantage**: Non-linear growth comes from duration, not intensity. "Painless repetition" builds the foundation.
3. **Difficulty is emergent**: Complexity is a by-product of the habit, not a starting condition.

## The "Painless Repetition" Strategy
- **The trap**: High standards + high intensity → failure → loss of confidence → collapse.
- **Suffering as a filter**: Suffering filters (experts survive, the fragile shatter); it does not forge. Imposing difficulty on a "fragile" system (a beginner) destroys it.
- **The solution**:
  - **Safe harbor**: Start from a place where success is guaranteed. Create a buffer against external harshness.
  - **Momentum first**: Focus on the habit loop, not on immediate results.
  - **Natural progression**: Increase difficulty only when the current level becomes trivial.

> **The core mantra**:
> Do not pursue intensity—pursue **painless repetition**.
> Painless repetition builds **momentum**.
> Momentum builds **identity**.
> A stable identity lets **difficulty** rise naturally.

## Applications
- **Skill acquisition**: Do not challenge yourself at first. Repeatedly master the basics until they become subconscious.
- **Self-compassion**: Imposing intensity leads to burnout. Prioritize "painless repetition" to build long-term capacity.

## The Certainty Trap

*Why people who put all their energy into "safe" things rarely achieve anything meaningful.*

Source: Preston, "What Kind of Person Will Most Likely Accomplish Nothing," May 2026.

### The Core Thesis

People who look like they are working hard but are not really progressing share one characteristic: **they put all their energy into the safest things**. They avoid uncertainty so thoroughly that they eliminate every possibility of a breakthrough.

### The Need for Cognitive Closure (Arie Kruglanski)

The human brain has an innate drive to resolve ambiguity and "close the door" on uncertain situations. When this need is too strong, it becomes **"certainty addiction"**—the brain systematically chooses tasks guaranteed to be completed but that produce no growth, rather than tasks that may fail but can bring a breakthrough.

### Three Manifestations
1. **Doing only what you are already good at**—skills stagnate, because real growth happens only in the "learning zone" (slightly beyond current ability).
2. **Always preparing, never starting**—reading books about a subject for years without trying. Preparation feels efficient but rationalizes avoiding the risk of failure.
3. **Frequently changing direction, never going deep**—jumping from AI to design to investing the moment difficulty appears. The beginner stage has the highest certainty and the fastest feedback, so serial switchers trap themselves in "always starting, never arriving."

> **Summary**: The root cause of mediocrity is seeking certainty to excess, and being unable to tolerate uncertainty to excess.

### Modern Amplifiers
- **Instant feedback loops** (social media, short videos) condition the brain to reject the "effort → long wait → unknown reward" pattern.
- **Anxiety amplified by comparison with others' success** strengthens the need for certainty. Every "safe" step leads down a path already walked—the most certain outcome is mediocrity.

### Breaking the Trap
1. **Set an "uncertainty quota"**—dedicate about 20% of your time to things with unknown outcomes. Use *graduated exposure therapy*: small, regular doses of uncertainty build tolerance over time. (The same mechanism as the track-switching practice in "Neuroplasticity" above: one exposes you to small doses of uncertainty at the schedule level, the other attaches a new action at the instant a behavior is triggered.)
2. **Shift from outcome goals to process goals**—not "write a bestseller" but "write 1,000 words a day." The process is 100% controllable; fear drops, action follows. *Make the process certain, let the outcome stay open.*
3. **Minimum viable experiments**—prototype before committing. Break uncertainty down into manageable probes: test a business idea with a weekend MVP; try writing 10 articles before declaring yourself a writer.

## Neuroplasticity: The Brain's Default State

*Why "I can be changed" is the wrong question—the real question is what I am being changed by.*

Source: Preston, "Once You Understand Neuroplasticity, Life Will Be Completely Different," October 2026.

### The Core Thesis

Plasticity is not a capability but the brain's default running state. It does not need to be started on purpose, and it does not distinguish between "practice time" and "non-practice time": what you do, what you think, and what you experience repeatedly all take part in shaping it. Change is therefore inevitable; the only thing you get to decide is what you are changed by.

The brain allocates resources by frequency of use—places you use more get a little more; places you stop using quietly have their resources taken back and moved elsewhere. Resources are finite, and it looks only at what you use most; it has no way to judge which functions you are not using now but which actually matter.

### Two Directions

- **Upward: use makes it stronger.** Neurons that fire together wire together—the more often two neurons are activated at the same time, the stronger the connection between them, and the easier it becomes for them to fire together again. After three months of learning to juggle, the region that processes visual-motion information showed a clear increase in gray matter; after three months without practice, part of that increase fell back. People who only "imagined" playing the piano in their heads showed changes in the motor cortex similar to those who practiced with a real instrument—mental rehearsal counts as input too.
- **Downward: not using is also a form of practice.** Stroke patients initially find the affected arm so effortful and so frustrating that they rely more and more on the healthy hand, and over time the affected arm truly becomes less and less responsive—Taub called this "learned non-use": not that it cannot be used, but that because it was not used, it eventually could not be. Constraint-induced movement therapy puts a mitt or sling on the healthy side and forces high-intensity daily use of the affected arm; its efficacy has been confirmed by multicenter clinical trials, and after treatment the corresponding region in the motor cortex expanded again. Chronic stress shapes the brain in the same way: rats kept under chronic stress for ten consecutive days showed amygdala neurons (alertness and fear) with longer, more branched dendrites, while some hippocampal neurons (learning and memory) actually had their dendrites shrink—the direction in which stress shapes the brain is precisely toward making it more prone to stress.

### Three Rewrites

- **"This is just who I am" → "So far, this is what my brain has been shaped into."** This is not merely consolation: a 2019 study in *Nature* covering more than 12,000 American ninth-graders, in which the students completed only two online lessons of just over twenty minutes each, whose core content was a single sentence—the brain changes through effort and practice. Over one school year, students who had been behind showed measurable gains, and overall the students became more willing to choose harder math courses. Merely knowing the fact that "the brain changes" is enough to change a person's choices.
- **"Persistence" is not a contest of willpower but an accumulation of repetitions—and not every repetition needs to be dramatic.** A group of neurons in the rat hippocampus that fired while the rat explored a field during the day fired again in a similar pattern during sleep, as if "replaying" the day's experience—after practice, the brain keeps processing. So twenty minutes a day for a month is usually far more effective than ten hours in a single burst: the former gives thirty chances to turn the switch on, plus thirty nights of tidying up.
- **The effortfulness of "difficulty" is the signal that the brain is remaking itself.** Methods that make the learning process more effortful—spaced practice, self-testing, interleaving—feel harder and produce worse performance in the moment, yet are remembered better and used more flexibly over time (Bjork's "desirable difficulty"). The next time you hit a "why is this so hard" moment, you can read it differently: it is not that I am incapable; my brain is under construction.

### Relation to "Painless Repetition"

"Desirable difficulty" here and "The Three Laws of Growth" / "The 'Painless Repetition' Strategy" above look opposed but in fact govern different links of the same process: painless repetition governs **whether this practice happens at all**, lowering the activation threshold and the psychological burden; desirable difficulty governs **whether this practice is effective**, deliberately making the practice more effortful once your capacity can carry it. A lower threshold does not mean the task itself has no difficulty—difficulty grows naturally, protected by the low threshold of the previous stage.

### Five Practices

1. **Run a repetition audit.** On a quiet evening, write down the five things you actually repeat most each day—not what you wish you did every day, but what you actually do. Then look at the list and ask: if the brain will make these five things more and more practiced, what kind of person will I be a year from now? This list is the training plan your brain is executing right now. This step requires no change; first see your daily behavioral pattern clearly.
2. **Use "small and focused" instead of "big and distracted."** Take twenty-five minutes a day for one thing you want to practice; put the phone in another room; do nothing else at the same time; stop when the time is up, even if you feel you could continue. Twenty-five minutes is a widely adopted unit of focus, and these twenty-five genuinely focused minutes beat two hours of practicing while playing.
3. **Deliberately make one thing "matter."** The brain changes only for what matters, so actively manufacture a reason for something to matter: connect it to a specific person (promise a friend you will explain what you learned next week), connect it to what you genuinely care about (learning English not for a streak, but to read primary sources in a field you are obsessed with), or set yourself a small external deadline. All of these are really answering one question for the brain: why is this thing worth changing me?
4. **After practicing, leave the brain some blank space.** People who closed their eyes and rested quietly for ten minutes after hearing a story remembered clearly more a week later than those who immediately went to play a spot-the-difference game. After learning something or practicing something, do not immediately pick up the phone; stare into space for a few minutes, or step outside for a walk—these few minutes look like nothing, but the brain is saving. Sleep matters for the same reason: studying late into the night often leaves the brain no time to archive.
5. **Do not fight old patterns head-on; try switching their track.** For patterns like rumination, habitual anxiety, or fleeing whenever something comes up, the first reaction for many people is to control themselves and not think about it, but the harder you struggle with it, the more you are attending to it—and therefore the more you are practicing it. A better way is to attach a new action the moment the old pattern starts: stand up and pour a glass of water, take ten deep breaths, or write the matter on paper and then go do the next thing. At first, blocking it one or two times out of ten is good enough; those one or two times are the first signals the new pathway receives. You do not need to destroy the old road; you only need to walk more new ones.

## By-Product Thinking

*Why "side hustles" fail, and what to do instead.*

### The Core Thesis

Most people treat a "side hustle" as **a second job in a completely different direction**—entirely separate from the main work. This is exhausting and rarely effective, because it doubles your workload without leveraging the momentum you already have.

**The truth: the best side-hustle output is a by-product of your main work.**

### Patterns
- **Knowledge sharing**: The problems you solved at work, the lessons you learned, the pitfalls you found—write them down. The thinking is already done; writing merely crystallizes it.
- **Open source**: Code you wrote for work, cleaned up and generalized. The hard part (design, testing, edge cases) is already done.
- **Wiki / documentation**: Record what you learn as you go (such as maintaining a personal knowledge base). The marginal cost of publishing is near zero, because the content already exists for your own use.

### The One-Week Rule (an operating principle)

> **A document not updated for over a week is ready to publish.**

Why one week:
- **It has settled**: Long enough to accumulate real insight, not just raw notes.
- **You have not forgotten it all**: Short enough that you still remember the context and can add nuance.
- **You can review and summarize**: The act of publishing forces a final review pass, which often turns up new connections.

### Why It Works
1. **Zero marginal thinking cost**: The intellectual work is done for your main job. Publishing is just formatting.
2. **It compounds with the main work**: Writing down what you learn deepens your own understanding, which makes you better at your main work. The "side" output feeds back into the main output.
3. **Sustainable**: No context-switching penalty. You are not maintaining two separate cognitive tracks.

### A Logical Derivation of Degrees of Freedom

**Why do some jobs produce by-products and others not?**

This is not random—it follows from first principles:

#### The Employer's Optimal Strategy

The employer wants to **minimize degrees of freedom and maximize control**. Degrees of freedom = unpredictability = risk. The ideal employee is fully replaceable and follows the SOP perfectly.

#### Why Degrees of Freedom Sometimes Exist

When **work complexity > SOP coverage**, the employer *must* grant degrees of freedom:
- Sales: every customer is different; you cannot script every interaction
- Engineering: unknown bugs require judgment; you cannot pre-specify every solution
- Consulting: every client has a unique problem; you cannot standardize the advice

No degrees of freedom → the work fails or cannot be outsourced.

#### Degrees of Freedom = Capability Redundancy

Degrees of freedom allow employees to develop capabilities **beyond the job requirements**:
- Sales: allowed to "maintain customer relationships" → build a personal network (redundancy)
- Engineers: allowed to "learn new technologies" → build transferable expertise (redundancy)

**Redundancy is the source of by-products.**

#### The Transferability Principle

> **There is no such thing as "a skill valuable to only one employer."**

All capabilities are transferable. The question is not *whether* they can be externalized, but *whether you have the degrees of freedom* to develop them.

**The weightlifter analogy**: A weightlifter can carry bricks. Likewise, if you can carry 10 times as many bricks as others, you can become a weightlifter. Capability is generic; the constraint is whether you are *allowed* to develop it.

#### The Logical Chain
```
High work complexity
  → cannot be fully specified by SOP
  → the employer must grant degrees of freedom (otherwise the work fails)
  → employees develop capabilities beyond the job requirements (redundancy)
  → redundancy can be externalized (by-products)
```

#### Verification: Why Some Jobs Have No By-Products

| Job | Complexity | SOP Coverage | Degrees of Freedom | Redundancy | By-Products |
|------|--------|---------|--------|------|--------|
| Assembly line | Low | 100% | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Military (operator)| Low | 100% (per protocol)| 0 (safety reinforces control)| 0 | 0 |
| Sales | High | Low (every customer differs)| High | High (customer network)| High |
| Engineering | High | Low (unknown problems)| High | High (technical expertise)| High |

**The truth about the military**: It is not that "secrecy blocks by-products"—it is that "the operator role has low complexity and can be SOP-ized; secrecy merely reinforces control." Military *researchers* (who need judgment) do produce by-products (papers, patents).

#### The Core Insight

> **Degrees of freedom are not "permission to externalize"—they are the physical manifestation of capability redundancy.**
>
> No degrees of freedom = capability fully specified by SOP = no redundancy = no by-products possible.

This is why the "by-product principle" applies to any job with sufficient degrees of freedom (sales, engineering, consulting, creative work), and does not apply to jobs with zero degrees of freedom (assembly lines, scripted call centers, military operators).

### The Optimal Strategy: Zero-Cost Iteration

**The logical extension**: If your job produces by-products, then the **marginal cost of doing a "side hustle" is near zero**.

#### The Cost Structure

| Method | Marginal Cost | Loss on Failure | Sustainability |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **By-product side hustle** | ≈ 0 (knowledge already exists)| Minimal (just formatting time)| **High** |
| Part-time | High (new context, new skills)| High (opportunity cost)| Low |
| Startup | Very high (capital, time, attention)| Catastrophic (financial ruin)| Very low |

#### Why By-Product Work Is Antifragile

1. **Cost ≈ 0**: The intellectual work is done for your main job. Publishing/formatting is trivial.
2. **Loss on failure ≈ 0**: If nobody reads your blog, you have lost 30 minutes. If your startup fails, you have lost 2 years + your savings.
3. **AI amplification**: AI can automate formatting, publishing, translating, and summarizing—further reducing the cost toward near zero.
4. **Compounding returns**: Small but continuous output → an accumulating audience → compounding opportunities (consulting, speaking, product launches).

#### The Optimal Strategy: Acting Through Non-Action

> **Just do it. Do not chase results.**

This is not philosophical advice—it is **mathematical optimization**:

- **High-cost ventures** (startups, side hustles): They require immediate ROI to justify the opportunity cost. This forces short-term thinking, cuts experiments off prematurely, and kills compounding growth.
- **Zero-cost by-products**: No ROI pressure. You can iterate for years with no results, and then compounding growth suddenly kicks in (the "hockey stick").

**The weightlifter analogy again**: If you can carry 10 times as many bricks, you *can* become a weightlifter. But the optimal strategy is not to quit your job and train full-time (high cost, high risk). It is to keep carrying bricks, post your training log online (zero cost), and let opportunities find you.

#### The Compounding Principle

> **First you earn interest. Then you earn compound interest.**

- **Interest**: Each by-product (a blog post, an open-source contribution, a wiki entry) is a small return on your daily work.
- **Compound interest**: Over time, these accumulate into reputation, network, and opportunity, producing returns *without additional effort*.

**The trap**: Trying to skip "interest" and jump straight to "compound interest" (such as launching a product before building an audience). This requires heavy upfront investment with uncertain returns—the opposite of antifragile.

### The Asymmetric Payoff Structure

The payoff matrix of the by-product model is naturally asymmetric (convex payoffs): the downside is limited—the loss on failure is only formatting time, and the process itself produces experience and understanding (a positive input); the upside is uncapped—the compounding growth of audience, reputation, and opportunity has no ceiling. It is similar to a long out-of-the-money option: the premium is extremely low, and once it hits, the payoff explodes. This is the same structure as the antifragility analysis in the previous section stated in two ways—antifragility says you grow stronger through volatility; asymmetric payoff says the expected value is structurally positive.

This structure also has a game-theoretic corollary: **when the cost of trial and error approaches zero, a non-mainstream strategy becomes the rational optimum**. Everyone walks the mainstream strategy (the involution equilibrium) because for high-cost participants failure is unbearable—they are forced to be conservative. The zero-cost experimenter does not have that constraint, and dares to explore directions others dare not touch; once a new strategy is found, the original equilibrium is broken, and the excess profit belongs to the first person to jump out of the game matrix. Being anti-mainstream is not a matter of taste; it is a cost-structure advantage.

Enthusiasm plays the role of a costly signal here: investment that disregards cost cannot itself be faked—being willing to keep investing at a stage with no expected return shows that the participant's failure floor is far higher than it appears. To collaborators, this is proof of reliability; to competitors, it is deterrence.

#### The Decision Rule

```
IF your job has degrees of freedom (capability redundancy exists)
  AND the marginal cost of externalizing ≈ 0
  AND AI can automate formatting/publishing
THEN:
  → Optimal strategy: just do it. Do not chase results. Always iterate.
  → Expected value: positive (low cost × high upside)
  → Risk: near zero (failure costs nothing)
ELSE:
  → You are in a "zero-degrees-of-freedom" job.
  → "Side hustle" = part-time job or startup (high cost, high risk).
  → Optimal strategy: quit and find a job with degrees of freedom, or accept having no by-products.
```

#### Why This Is Not a "Passive Income" Fantasy

This is not "getting rich while you sleep." It is **an asymmetric bet**:
- **Downside**: capped at near zero (the time you spend formatting by-products).
- **Upside**: unlimited (viral content, job offers, consulting opportunities, product launches).

The math is simple: if you have 100 zero-cost experiments and 1 goes wildly successful, you win. The cost of the 99 failures is negligible.

**Compared with a startup**: 1 startup = 1 bet. If it fails, you are finished. You cannot afford 100 startups. But you can afford 100 blog posts.

#### The Meta-Principle

> **Zero-cost iteration + compounding time = inevitable success.**
>
> High-cost iteration + impatience = inevitable burnout.

This is why "side-hustle culture" fails: it treats by-product work as startup work (high effort, high risk, immediate ROI). The correct framing is **gardening, not hunting**—sow every day, never harvest (let compounding growth do the work).

## The Source of Judgment: The Real Meaning of Offline Communication

### The Core Proposition

The meaning of offline communication **lies not in the communication itself**, but in **cultivating familiarity with human nature**, and thereby improving **judgment**.

In the age of AI, judgment is the **only commercially valuable capability** a person has.

### Case: A Client Revises 10 Times and Finally Picks Draft 1

Three possibilities:
1. Lu Da toying with Zheng Tu (deliberately jerking you around)
2. The client is dissatisfied with draft 1, but even more dissatisfied with the other 9, and with the deadline arriving can only pick draft 1
3. **The client did not figure out what he wanted at the start, and gradually discovered his needs through the communication** (the most likely case; 60% of clients are like this)

The third is the norm. Nothing else—it is the result of big-data statistics.

### The Propagation Effect: Cognitive Bias Online vs Offline

If you live online every day, you will feel that everyone in the world is extreme (gender conflict, melodramatic plots). But living offline, normal people are the majority.

**The reason**: the propagation effect. A dog biting a man does not make the trending list; a man biting a dog does. An ordinary life of marrying, having children, and getting by has no talking-point appeal; melodrama does.

If your life experience is limited to the internet, the melodrama from every nook and cranny of the world is gathered and moved in front of you to play daily, and you come to understand the world as a basin of melodrama, and you become a troll too.

**A summer insect cannot speak of ice**: without having seen a sufficient sample, judgment becomes disordered.

### Social Differences Online/Offline and Inside/Outside the System

- **Not yet in the system**: Colleague relationships scatter once every 3 years, each going their own way, and you do not give wedding cash gifts (they cannot be recovered)
- **In the system**: Colleague relationships are stable long-term, and you do give wedding cash gifts (they will pay it back + interest)

Online and offline are different, and inside and outside the system are different too. When the dimensions of observation are not enough, people place particular weight on "the other person understands me"—if they understand, you are as happy as if you had seen Jesus; if they do not, you are sad/angry and endlessly arguing your case.

### The Trait of the Old Hand: Not Caring

> **The willow catkins on the branch are blown ever fewer; at the ends of the earth, where is there no fragrant grass? It is the passionate who are vexed by the passionless.**

> If you care, you have lost.

The true masters are all **the ones who do not care**, because they are **going through the motions**.

Many people resent officialese / buck-passing / going through the motions. But if these did not work, how would they have survived several thousand years and stood unshaken? Answer: they work too well. So well that they cannot be openly encouraged, yet everyone quietly practices them.

If you take everything literally and argue hard to win, who is most annoyed by you? **Your boss is most annoyed by you.**

### The Definition of Maturity

> A mature person goes out to do business, does not invest emotion in business, and **does whatever is most effective**.

> Do you actually want to **win**? Or do you want to **argue about who wins**?

What the boss wants: you sign the deal and earn the money; that is the goal. Do not drag in whether clients/colleagues understand you. If you like seeking understanding, spend money on a psychologist; do not hold up everyone else's earning.

What a mature colleague wants: **expanding profit** (wanting to win) + **controlling risk** (reducing arguing about who wins).

### The Ultimate Insight: Everyone Has a Switch

After seeing much and knowing much, you realize this is just human nature. The contrarian streak in you disappears, because you suddenly discover: **there are actually no living people in this world**.

Everyone has a **switch**; turn the switch the right way and that person will follow you obediently, just like a robot. So your mind gradually shifts to the switches, and you no longer have any interest in those wretched matters of arguing about who wins / begging to be understood.

**Everyone has a point that can be gripped; grip the seven inches, seize the crux, and the world is yours to roam.**

Then where would you still get your "2B" air / contrarian air from? Only people who live miserably switch between servility and thunderous rage. Those who hold victory in hand are always **light as clouds and breeze**.

### Grounding: Practicing from an Early Age

To become a seasoned hand who handles things with ease, an expert at manipulating others' expectations, what is needed is precisely **to practice from an early age, to practice by getting along with people**.

Seeing much and knowing much → understanding human nature → finding the switch → no longer agonizing over being understood → light as clouds and breeze.

This skill point of holding victory in hand is cultivated, one item at a time, out of offline communication.
